ABSTRACT 
 
Medication management can be challenging for elderly individuals and long-
term medication users who need to manage multiple medication schedules, 
dose adherence, treatment duration, medicine stock, and health records. 
When these activities are handled separately, maintaining a consistent 
medication routine can become difficult. MediBuddy is an Android-based 
medication adherence and personal health management system designed to 
integrate these activities into a single, elderly-oriented workflow. The 
proposed system consists of six modules covering prescription onboarding, 
medication adherence, health monitoring, medication knowledge and safety, 
stock management, and personal health records. A key feature of MediBuddy 
is the integration of dose-level adherence with medicine-stock management. 
Each scheduled dose is tracked individually, and stock is reduced only when 
the corresponding dose is confirmed as Taken, allowing stock information to 
reflect recorded consumption rather than scheduled usage. The system also 
supports medication-course management, refill awareness, multi-parameter 
health logging, local health history, and shareable health reports. Core 
functions operate locally on the device, reducing dependence on continuous 
Internet connectivity. Future extensions include verified prescription 
digitization, Indian medication knowledge and safety support, and personal 
health-record management. MediBuddy provides an integrated framework 
for medication adherence, stock management, and personal health monitoring 
without providing medical diagnosis or treatment recommendations

CHAPTER 1 
INTRODUCTION 
1.1 MOTIVATION 
Medication routines become increasingly difficult to manage when a 
person takes several medicines, follows multiple daily timings, combines 
short prescribed courses with long-term medicines, or needs to remember 
whether a particular scheduled dose was actually confirmed. These tasks are 
especially relevant for elderly users, for whom a simple and consistent 
interaction flow can reduce the routine effort involved in organizing 
medication information. 
Conventional paper notes, alarm clocks and calendar reminders can 
provide useful prompts, but a reminder alone does not maintain a structured 
relationship between a medication, a specific scheduled dose occurrence, the 
user’s confirmation, the resulting adherence record, the actual medicine 
consumed, the remaining stock and the applicable course duration. Health 
measurements and reports are also frequently maintained separately. 
MediBuddy is motivated by the need to connect these activities within one 
Android application rather than treating them as isolated tasks. 
The engineering focus of MediBuddy is therefore not the novelty of an 
alarm, stock counter or health log individually. Its contribution lies in an 
integrated workflow in which structured medication data leads to schedules 
and local reminders; user confirmation creates persistent occurrence-level 
adherence information; confirmed Taken events drive physical stock 
reduction; course information affects future scheduling and refill assessment; 
and health readings can be recorded, reviewed and exported as reports. The

interface is intended to remain clear and elderly-oriented, with core 
implemented functions relying on deterministic local processing rather than

1.2 EXISTING SYSTEM 
Existing medication-management practices span manual paper records, 
ordinary smartphone alarms and calendars, dedicated medication reminder 
applications, pharmacy and health-ecosystem applications, and separate 
health-record or monitoring applications. These approaches are useful in 
different contexts. Calendar and alarm applications can notify users at chosen 
times, while commercial health applications may offer combinations of 
reminders, refill support, medicine information, ordering or health records. 
Therefore, it would be inaccurate to state that such functions do not exist 
elsewhere. 
The practical limitation addressed by this project is fragmentation. A 
generic alarm may indicate that it is time for a medicine but does not 
inherently create a medication-specific occurrence record or connect that 
confirmation to actual stock consumption. Likewise, a separate stock note 
may not know whether a scheduled dose was Taken, Skipped or left 
unconfirmed. Health measurements and reports can also exist outside the 
medication workflow. MediBuddy proposes a consolidated, elderly-oriented 
workflow in which these data relationships are explicit. 
The current implementation remains intentionally local for core 
medication, adherence, stock and health-record functions. Optional online 
medicine search and future network-dependent functions are treated 
separately. The project does not claim that every individual feature is 
unprecedented; the design emphasis is the integration and consistency of the 
complete workflow.

1.3 PROBLEM STATEMENT 
Elderly individuals and long-term medication users may need to 
manage medication schedules, dose confirmation, remaining medicine stock 
and health measurements simultaneously. Conventional reminder 
mechanisms can notify users at specified times but generally do not, by 
themselves, form a complete medication-management workflow in which 
each scheduled dose is linked to adherence status, actual stock consumption, 
prescribed-course information and health records. Fragmented handling of 
these activities can make medication and health-record management difficult. 
Therefore, there is a need for an integrated, elderly-oriented system that 
combines structured medication scheduling, dose-level adherence recording, 
consumption-based stock management, multi-parameter health logging and 
report management while providing an extensible foundation for verified 
prescription digitization, medication knowledge and personal health records.

1.5 PROPOSED SYSTEM 
MediBuddy is proposed as a six-module Android medication 
adherence, medication management and personal health monitoring system. 
The complete design includes prescription onboarding, medication dose and 
adherence management, multi-parameter health monitoring and reporting, 
Indian medication knowledge and safety, stock and refill management, and 
personal health-record / document management. At the current review stage, 
--- PAGE ---
=== Page 19 ===
 6 
Modules 2, 3 and 5 are implemented; Modules 1, 4 and 6 remain planned 
Phase-2 work. 
The implemented architecture follows Jetpack Compose UI → 
ViewModel → Repository → Room Database, with Android platform 
services used for exact alarms, notifications, broadcast handling, boot-related 
rescheduling, PDF generation and sharing. A medication can contain multiple 
daily times and can be ongoing or fixed-duration. Each scheduled occurrence 
can progress from Due to Taken or Skipped; if no Taken/Skipped 
confirmation is received within the configured 60-minute grace period, the 
application records it as Missed. This state means that no confirmation was 
received and does not medically prove that the medicine was not consumed. 
Stock is represented independently from adherence but is updated 
through adherence events: only a confirmed Taken occurrence can reduce 
physical stock. Unknown stock is allowed and displayed as “Stock not set.” 
The health module stores manually entered measurements locally and applies 
informational reference processing where applicable. Reports are generated 
locally and shared through standard Android mechanisms. Future 
prescription processing will distinguish digital text extraction from OCR and 
will require user verification before extracted medication information can 
affect schedules. 
1.6 BENEFITS OF THE PROJECT 
• Integrated Medication and Health Management 
 MediBuddy brings medication scheduling, dose adherence tracking, 
medicine stock management, course information, health-parameter 
logging, and report generation into a single Android application. 
Instead of maintaining these activities separately through alarms, 
paper notes, or different applications, the system provides a 
--- PAGE ---
=== Page 20 ===
 7 
structured workflow in which medication and health information 
can be managed together. This is particularly useful for elderly users 
and people following long-term or multiple-medication routines. 
• Improved Medication Routine Management 
   The application provides scheduled local medication reminders and 
maintains records for individual dose occurrences. Users can 
confirm a scheduled dose as Taken or Skipped, while an occurrence 
for which no confirmation is received within the configured grace 
period is recorded as Missed for application tracking. This reduces 
dependence on memory alone and provides a persistent history of 
medication-related actions without claiming that the application can 
medically verify whether a medicine was consumed. 
• Consumption-Based Medication Stock Tracking 
 A major benefit of the system is the connection between dose 
confirmation and physical medicine stock. Stock is reduced only 
after a dose is confirmed as Taken, while Skipped, Missed, or 
unconfirmed doses do not cause stock deduction. This provides a 
more meaningful representation of actual medicine consumption 
than simply reducing stock according to the prescribed schedule. 
The system also supports unknown stock through the “Stock not set” 
state rather than assuming that the user begins with a complete strip 
or package.
