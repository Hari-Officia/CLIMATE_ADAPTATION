1.
Poopradubsil, T. & Thaipisutikul, T., "ClimatePolicyGen: A Multi-Agent Framework for Climate Policy Generation Using Multivariate and Multimodal Time Series Inputs," Intelligent Systems with Applications, Vol. 30, 2026.
Proposes ClimatePolicyGen, a multi-agent framework that employs Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate climate policies by integrating environmental, socio-economic, and infrastructure time-series data for decision support
The framework improved policy coherence by 12.3% and policy relevance by 18.7% compared with baseline approaches. It successfully generated region-specific climate adaptation and mitigation policies using five specialized AI agents.
Integrate real-time climate data, strengthen policy validation through expert evaluation, and extend the framework for global climate governance and national decision-support applications.
2.
Shan, S., "ClimateAgents: A Multi-Agent Research Assistant for Social-Climate Dynamics Analysis," arXiv Preprint, 2026
Introduces ClimateAgents, a collaborative multi-agent system that combines data retrieval, statistical analysis, hypothesis generation, and automated reasoning to support climate research and socio-environmental decision making.
The specialized AI agents effectively analyzed relationships between climate indicators and socio-economic variables, producing interpretable analytical reports and supporting adaptive climate research workflows.
Expand the framework with additional climate datasets, policy simulation modules, and real-time environmental monitoring to support climate adaptation planning.
3.
Schwabe, M., et al., "Quantum Machine Learning for Climate Modelling," IEEE International Conference on Quantum Artificial Intelligence (QAI), 2025.
Develops Quantum Machine Learning (QML) models using Quantum Neural Networks to improve climate modelling and Earth System prediction by efficiently processing high-dimensional climate datasets.
The proposed QML framework demonstrated promising improvements in computational efficiency and prediction accuracy for complex climate simulations, highlighting the potential of quantum computing in climate science.
Validate hybrid quantum-classical models on large-scale real-world climate datasets and deploy them on fault-tolerant quantum hardware for operational climate forecasting.
4.
Schwabe, M., et al., "Opportunities and Challenges of Quantum Computing for Climate Modelling," arXiv Preprint, 2025.
Reviews the applications of quantum computing in climate modelling, optimization, uncertainty quantification, and environmental decision support while discussing current technological limitations.
Quantum optimization techniques can significantly accelerate climate simulations and improve decision-making, although current hardware scalability and quantum noise remain major challenges.
Develop scalable quantum algorithms and benchmark them against advanced classical climate models for practical climate adaptation and mitigation planning.
5.
Gürcan, Ö., et al., "A Role-Based Multi-Agent Model for Climate Adaptation Deliberation Across Living Labs," AIAA SciTech Forum, 2026.
Develops a role-based multi-agent architecture consisting of Expert, Evaluator, Disseminator, and Decision-Maker agents to facilitate collaborative climate adaptation planning among stakeholders.
The framework improved transparency, stakeholder collaboration, and decision quality while enabling reusable climate adaptation strategies across different Living Lab case studies.
Validate the framework using real-world climate adaptation projects and integrate intelligent decision-support modules for automated policy planning.
 6.
"A Multi-Agent Decision-Making Framework for Evaluating Water and Environmental Resources Management Scenarios Under Climate Change," Science of the Total Environment, 2022.
Presents a multi-agent decision-support framework that combines game theory and environmental resource management to evaluate climate adaptation strategies under uncertain climate conditions.
The framework successfully optimized water resource allocation and environmental management decisions while balancing stakeholder objectives under climate change scenarios.
Integrate AI-based optimization algorithms, larger climate datasets, and real-time monitoring systems to improve climate adaptation and mitigation planning.
7.
"A RAG-Based Multi-Agent LLM System for Natural Hazard Resilience and Adaptation (WildfireGPT)," arXiv Preprint, 2025.
Develops a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)-based Multi-Agent LLM framework that integrates climate projections, scientific literature, and hazard information to support climate adaptation and disaster resilience planning.
The multi-agent framework generated more accurate, explainable, and context-aware recommendations than conventional single-agent AI systems, improving decision support for natural hazard adaptation.
Expand the framework to support additional climate hazards such as floods, droughts, cyclones, and wildfire mitigation while integrating live environmental monitoring.
8.
“Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning Based Decision Support Model for Resilient Community Post-Hazard Recovery,” Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence, 2025.
Uses a multi-period optimization model with a Proximal Policy Optimization-based multi-agent DRL approach to allocate relief goods efficiently and equitably during the critical 72-hour disaster response window.
The DRL-based decision support approach improved the efficiency, effectiveness, and equity of relief distribution compared to conventional allocation methods under uncertainty.
Extending the model to multi-hazard and multi-region relief coordination scenarios.
9.
Bihlo, A., et al., "Sub-seasonal Precipitation Forecasting Using Transformer Networks," ETH Zurich Research Collection, 2024.
This paper proposes a Transformer-based deep learning model for sub-seasonal precipitation forecasting using climate reanalysis data. The model captures long-term spatial and temporal weather patterns to improve rainfall prediction and support climate adaptation planning.
The proposed model achieved better forecasting accuracy than conventional LSTM and CNN models. It effectively predicted long-term rainfall patterns while reducing forecasting errors across different climate regions.
Future research aims to integrate satellite observations, improve prediction of extreme rainfall events, and develop real-time forecasting systems for disaster management.
10.
"Exploring Equity of Climate Policies Using Multi-Agent Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning," arXiv Preprint, 2025.
Presents a Multi-Agent Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning framework that evaluates climate policies by balancing emissions reduction, economic development, and social equity to support sustainable climate decision making.
The framework produced climate policy recommendations that achieved better trade-offs between environmental sustainability and socio-economic objectives compared with conventional optimization approaches.
Extend the framework with real-time environmental data, larger socio-economic datasets, and government policy evaluation systems to support national climate adaptation and mitigation planning.
11.
“Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Resources Allocation Optimization: A Survey,” Artificial Intelligence Review (Springer), 2025.
Comprehensively reviews MARL algorithms (MADQN, MAPPO, MAAC, MADDPG) applied to resource-allocation problems across energy, IoT, and disaster-resilience domains.
Decentralized MARL approaches effectively handle real-time, distributed resource-allocation control in dynamic environments such as smart grids and resilient smart-farm task allocation (MaaRS).
Standardizing MARL benchmarks and improving sample efficiency for resource-constrained deployment.
12.
Gürcan, Ö. et al., “A Role-Based Multi-Agent Model for Climate Adaptation Deliberation Across Living Labs,” AIAA SciTech Forum, 2026.
A role-based Multi-Agent Computer Model in which stakeholder agents (Expert Evaluator, Disseminator, Positioning Agent, Decision-Maker) simulate climate-adaptation deliberation across four phases.
The configurable, role-based architecture improved interpretability and reusability of adaptation-governance simulations across different Living Lab case studies.
Validating the model against empirical deliberation data from multiple real Living Labs.
13.
“A RAG-Based Multi-Agent LLM System for Natural Hazard Resilience and Adaptation” (WildfireGPT), arXiv preprint, 2025.
A user-centered multi-agent RAG system integrating hazard projections, observational data, and literature to deliver tailored wildfire adaptation and risk insights.
Evaluation across ten expert-led case studies showed the multi-agent design significantly outperformed existing single-model LLM decision-support tools for hazard resilience.
Extending the multi-agent architecture to additional hazard types such as floods and hurricanes.
14.
Nandan, M. & Gupta, S.K., “Interpretable Machine Learning Models for Early Climate Risk Forecasting and Disaster Mitigation,” SGS – Engineering & Sciences, 1(3), 2025.
Combines predictive models (Random Forest, XGBoost, LSTM) with explainable AI methods (SHAP, LIME, counterfactuals) in an interpretable ML framework for multi-hazard climate risk forecasting.
A PRISMA-guided review (2018–2025) shows XAI methods improve trust and usability of climate-risk models but reveals persistent gaps in scalability and multi-hazard integration.
Improving stakeholder usability of explanations and integrating multi-hazard interpretable models into operational disaster-management workflows.
15.
Lu, W., Qi, S., Zheng, B. et al., “Optimisation Study of Carbon Dioxide Geological Storage Sites Based on GIS and Machine Learning Algorithms,” Geomechanics and Geophysics for Geo-Energy and Geo-Resources (Springer), 11, 19, 2025.
Integrates GIS spatial analysis with machine learning models, including XGBoost and deep neural networks, to optimize CO₂ geological storage site selection.
All five ML models reached about 95% prediction precision, with the deep neural network model achieving the highest precision (96.4%) for site-selection screening.
Extending the GIS-ML framework to offshore storage sites and incorporating long-term leakage-risk monitoring.
16.
Li, Y., et al., "Geospatial Machine Learning for High-Resolution Flood Mapping," International Journal of Applied Earth Observation and Geoinformation, 2025.
This paper proposes a geospatial machine learning framework for high-resolution flood mapping using satellite imagery, GIS data, and terrain information. The model aims to improve flood risk assessment and support climate adaptation and disaster management.
The proposed model accurately identified flood-prone areas with higher spatial resolution than traditional mapping methods. It improved flood susceptibility analysis and provided reliable information for emergency planning and environmental decision support.
Future work focuses on integrating real-time satellite data, climate projections, and Explainable AI techniques to enhance flood prediction accuracy and support sustainable climate adaptation planning.
17.
Jensen, H., & Nielsen, C., "Dynamic Policy Simulation and Adaptive Climate Mitigation Frameworks Using Deep Reinforcement Learning," Environmental Modeling & Assessment, 2025.
This paper proposes a Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based framework for adaptive climate mitigation planning. The model continuously learns from environmental and policy data to recommend optimal mitigation strategies for reducing greenhouse gas emissions.
The RL framework successfully adapted mitigation strategies under changing climate conditions and outperformed conventional optimization methods. It demonstrated improved decision-making by dynamically selecting actions that reduced emissions while maintaining sustainable development goals.
Future research aims to integrate real-time climate datasets, multi-agent reinforcement learning, and explainable AI techniques to support national and regional climate mitigation planning.
18.
Long-Term Climate Mitigation and Policy Evaluation Frameworks Through the Integration of Deep Machine Learning with Integrated Assessment Models, Nature Climate Change, 2025.
This paper integrates Deep Machine Learning with Integrated Assessment Models (IAMs) to improve climate impact prediction, policy evaluation, and long-term mitigation planning. The approach combines climate, economic, and energy system data for comprehensive decision support.
The proposed framework improved prediction accuracy and computational efficiency compared to traditional IAMs. It effectively analyzed multiple climate scenarios and supported policymakers in selecting sustainable adaptation and mitigation strategies.
Future work includes incorporating real-time climate observations, socio-economic indicators, and quantum computing techniques to develop more accurate and scalable climate decision-support systems.
19.
Wang, Y., et al., "Evacuation Dynamics Using Agent-Based Modeling for Urban Disaster Management," Springer Urban Computing Journal, 2025.
This paper develops an Agent-Based Modeling (ABM) framework to simulate human evacuation behavior during natural disasters. The model supports emergency planning by analyzing crowd movement, evacuation routes, and disaster response strategies.
The simulation improved evacuation efficiency by identifying optimal routes and reducing congestion during emergency situations. The model also helped decision-makers evaluate different disaster response policies under various hazard scenarios.
Future research will integrate real-time sensor data, GIS-based hazard mapping, and Multi-Agent AI techniques to enhance emergency response and climate adaptation planning.
20.
Ikendi, S., Lyons, A., & Pathak, T. B., "Advancing Decision Support for Climate Adaptation in Agriculture and Natural Resources," Frontiers in Environmental Science, Vol. 13, 2025.
This paper presents a decision-support framework for climate adaptation in agriculture and natural resource management using climate data and stakeholder inputs.
The framework improved climate adaptation planning by enhancing data accessibility, supporting informed decision-making, and increasing stakeholder participation.
Future work focuses on integrating AI techniques, real-time climate data, and advanced decision-support tools for sustainable climate adaptation.
